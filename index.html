01 / A little about me

I like the messy middle — where an idea meets the reality of play.

I’m a Game Technology student at Politeknik Elektronika Negeri Surabaya. Across team projects and solo work, I’ve designed mechanics, built gameplay systems, shaped interfaces, assembled environments, and explored what makes player interactions feel clear, tense, satisfying, or surprising.

I enjoy moving across disciplines: starting with a player experience, making it tangible in-engine, trying it out, noticing what feels off, and finding a practical next iteration. I’m especially interested in gameplay systems, narrative choices, horror atmosphere, readable interfaces, and the small details that shape how a game is experienced.

02 / My practice

Different tools.
One player experience.

I don’t see design, implementation, and evaluation as isolated steps. They inform each other. These are the areas I’ve explored through real game projects so far.

01 / Design

Shape the experience

Mechanics, encounter pacing, player flow, narrative dilemmas, and the feeling a game is trying to create.

02 / Systems

Make it work

Gameplay implementation and iteration in Unity and Godot, from resource-driven floors to physics-based interactions and dialogue systems.

03 / Interface

Make it readable

UI/UX decisions across menus, HUDs, dialogue presentation, and result screens—balancing clarity, mood, and available scope.

04 / Evaluation

Question the result

Playtesting during development, observing unexpected behavior, troubleshooting mechanics, reviewing static QA findings, and recording limitations.

04 / How I work

Curiosity first.
Then make it real.

My process is practical and iterative. The exact steps change with the project, but I tend to move between these four questions.

01

What should the player feel or understand?

Start with the intended experience, not just the feature list.

02

What is the simplest way to try it?

Prototype the idea and keep scope realistic for the time and tools available.

03

What actually happens in play?

Try the interaction, look for friction, and separate observed outcomes from assumptions.

04

What is worth changing next?

Refine the part that matters most, while being honest about limitations and trade-offs.
